# Logo brief: Deepak Bojjagani

Use this brief to design a logo for Deepak Bojjagani's personal brand. The logo must fit the portfolio website described below, which is already built and live. Read the whole brief before generating anything.

Live site: https://deepak6007.github.io/Deepak_Portfolio/

---

## 1. Who this is for

**Deepak Bojjagani** is an automotive and industrial designer based in Bengaluru, India. He also works as a product photographer.

- **Main skill:** Class-A surface modelling in Autodesk Alias. That means building the smooth, continuous body panels of cars and products, then checking them with "zebra stripe" reflection analysis until the highlights run clean.
- **Also does:** solid modelling in Fusion 360, scenes and renders in Blender and KeyShot, sketching, 3D printing and physical prototypes, and product photography on a Canon EOS 90D.
- **Experience:**
  - Maruti Suzuki: design intern, alloy and steel wheels.
  - Heed Jetboards: industrial designer, jetboard hand controller.
  - AS Dream Studio: photography intern.
- **Education:** Industrial and Product Design, M. S. Ramaiah University of Applied Sciences (2021–2025).
- **Portfolio projects:** a sports-bike concept, a Dodge Challenger surface model, a turbine-spoke alloy wheel, and a jetboard joystick controller.

**Who sees the logo:** freelance clients (brands that need 3D models, renders or product photos) and recruiters at automotive and product design studios.

**What the logo should say:** "This person builds clean, precise surfaces and understands how light falls on them." It should look professional and engineered, not decorative.

---

## 2. Brand personality

| Is | Is not |
|---|---|
| Precise, engineered | Playful, cartoonish |
| Calm, confident | Loud, aggressive, "racing" |
| Studio-lit, material | Glowing, neon, futuristic |
| Minimal, purposeful | Ornamental, busy |
| Automotive by craft | Automotive by cliché |

Think of the visual world of a car design studio: clay models under long strip lights, a highlight line running unbroken along a body panel, zebra stripes on a surface, a technical drawing with a single section line.

---

## 3. The website's visual system (the logo must sit inside this)

### Colours

| Role | Name | Hex | Use in the logo |
|---|---|---|---|
| Background | Graphite | `#1A1A1A` | Main background the logo appears on |
| Surface | Card | `#212120` | Secondary dark background |
| Primary ink | Studio grey | `#ECECEA` | Main logo colour on dark backgrounds |
| Muted | Soft grey | `#A6A6A2` | Secondary text, never the main mark |
| Hairline | Line | `#3A3A3A` | Thin rules and borders |
| Accent | Signal orange | `#E2501D` | At most one small detail, such as a dot or short bar. Never a fill or gradient. |

Project tints are used elsewhere on the site (bike red `#C8312A`, bronze `#9C6E43`, blue `#4F6FB0`, teal `#2E7F8C`). **Do not use them in the logo.**

The site is dark first. The logo must work as:
1. Studio grey on graphite (primary)
2. Graphite on studio grey or white (light version)
3. Pure black or pure white, one colour (stamps, embossing, watermarks on photos)

### Typography

- **Website font:** Barlow (Google Fonts), weights 400, 500 and 600. It's a grotesk drawn from California highway signage: slightly rounded, open and readable.
- **Headlines:** semi-bold (600), tight letter spacing, sentence case.
- **The wordmark** can use Barlow, a close relative (for example Barlow Semi Condensed), or custom lettering that sits comfortably next to Barlow.
- **Avoid** these overused fonts: Inter, Instrument Sans, IBM Plex, DM Sans, Space Grotesk, Outfit, Plus Jakarta Sans, Montserrat, Poppins.

### Shapes and surfaces on the site

- Solid dark cards with a 1px hairline border and small corner radii (8–14 px). No glass, blur or glow.
- Thin 1px lines are the main graphic device: a horizontal "floor line" behind the 3D bike, and a vertical scroll line in the margin with a small dot that moves as you scroll.
- The home page shows an interactive 3D model of the sports bike in a framed studio box.
- The photography page shows photos in white polaroid frames on charcoal.

## 4. What to design

### Required pieces

1. **Wordmark:** "Deepak Bojjagani" on one line. Also a stacked version on two lines (Deepak / Bojjagani).
2. **Monogram:** a compact mark from the initials **D** and **B**. It's used for the favicon, social profile picture and photo watermark.
3. **Lock-up:** monogram plus wordmark, horizontal.

### Size and use requirements

- The monogram must be readable at **16 × 16 px** (browser tab icon) and at **32 × 32 px**. Test it small: thin details that disappear at 16 px are a fail.
- It must work in **one flat colour**, with no gradients, shadows, bevels or 3D effects.
- Simple geometric construction that can be redrawn cleanly as an **SVG vector**.
- It must sit in the left of a 60 px-tall dark navigation bar next to text links, without feeling heavy.
- It must work as a small watermark in the corner of product photos.

---

## 5. Concept directions to explore

These ideas come from the subject's own work. Explore at least three and show them side by side.

1. **Highlight line:** a single continuous line, like the reflection that runs along a well-surfaced car panel. It bends smoothly through the D and B, or underlines the wordmark. It should suggest "continuous curvature" (G2/G3 surfaces) without drawing a car.
2. **Zebra stripes:** two or three parallel bands that flow through the monogram, as if the letters were being checked with zebra analysis. The bands must be even and smooth, with no kinks.
3. **Section line:** the monogram treated like a technical drawing. Clean construction lines, one section cut, a precise corner radius. Think engineering drawing, not blueprint cliché.
4. **Studio strip light:** a short horizontal bar above or below the mark, like the long strip lights over a clay model. This could be where the one signal-orange detail goes.
5. **Surface transition:** the D's curve flowing into the B's curve with one continuous, tangent-matched transition, so the two letters read as one moulded form.

The current placeholder is "DB" in Arial Black on a dark rounded square, with a short orange bar underneath. It's only a stand-in; replace it, don't refine it.

---

## 6. Avoid

- Car or bike silhouettes, wheels, steering wheels, speedometers, chequered flags, tyre tracks, wings, flames.
- Camera icons, aperture blades, lens rings or film strips (photography is secondary).
- Gears, wrenches, pencils, compasses, light bulbs, or any "designer" stock icon.
- Gradients, chrome, glow, neon, glass, metallic textures or drop shadows.
- Initials inside a plain circle or hexagon badge.
- Swooshes, speed lines and italics that suggest "fast".
- Handwritten script, signature-style logos, serif "luxury" monograms.
- Mascots, faces or caricatures.
- More than one accent colour, or orange as the main colour.

## 7. Deliverables to show

For each concept:

1. The monogram large, on graphite `#1A1A1A`, in studio grey `#ECECEA`.
2. The same monogram at 64 px, 32 px and 16 px, side by side.
3. The horizontal lock-up (monogram plus "Deepak Bojjagani").
4. The light version (graphite on `#ECECEA`).
5. A one-colour black version.
6. A mock-up of the website nav bar: dark bar, logo on the left, "Work · Services · About · Photography" links, and a "Start a project" button on the right.
7. One sentence explaining the idea behind the concept.

Keep shapes flat and simple so the chosen concept can be redrawn as a clean SVG afterwards.

## 9. After a concept is chosen

Image generators usually can't spell names correctly or produce clean vectors. Treat the generated images as concept sketches. The chosen direction will be redrawn as SVG by hand, with the wordmark set in real Barlow text, so the final files are sharp at every size. Those files are the favicon, nav logo, social avatar, photo watermark and Open Graph image.
